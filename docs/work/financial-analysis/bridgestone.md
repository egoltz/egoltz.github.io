# Bridgestone Behavioral Health - Case Analysis
_Analysis in Excel_
Bridgestone Behavioral Health Center is a vital provider of substance abuse treatment and mental
health services, but faces ongoing financial instability. Despite budgeting for a small profit, the
organization operated at a loss last year and projects a only a small profit for the following year.
Leadership lacks the financial tools and expertise to effectively analyze cost behavior, monitor service
volume, or understand the drivers of profitability, limiting its ability to make informed operational
decisions.
For the upcoming year, Bridgestone has projected $5,000,000 in revenue across 10 services, with
$1,500,000 in variable costs. While the high contribution margin indicates strong profit potential per unit
of service, this margin can only be realized if targeted service volumes are met. With current operating
income projected at just $6,300, even a small decline in service revenue or a slight increase in fixed or
variable costs could push Bridgestone into a financial deficit.
Without improved financial oversight and planning, Bridgestone risks continued underperformance,
threatening its ability to deliver essential services and support the broader well-being of the community
it serves.

## Risks Analysis for Q1
Bridgestone Behavioral Health operates on tight margins and is highly sensitive to changes in
service volume, pricing (reimbursement rates), and fixed costs. Group counseling is one of
Bridgestone’s highest-volume and high-margin services. A 10% decline in service volume or a
10% Decrease in Medicare reimbursement rate would cut Q1 revenue by $22,250, directly
reducing contribution margin and pushing the organization into deficit. A 1% increase in fixed
costs raises the breakeven threshold significantly. Given the already slim operating
margin (~$6,300), this increase would wipe out the entire profit and deepen losses.
![Bridgestone Profit/Loss](/assets/images/bridge-profit-loss.png)
## Evaluating Bridgestone Services: Revenue Contributions
Most of the crucial services operate a relatively high Contribution Margin Ratio. This means that when these
services increase, the profit increase rapidly, but if service levels decrease unexpectedly it means significant decline in revenue.
![Bridgestone Contribution Margin % by Service](/assets/images/bridge-service-cmr.png)
## Fixed Cost Analysis
Costs such as Salaries & Benefits would be difficult to cut without cuts to services. Some fixed costs could be cut and essential services would not be profoundly affected. A 10% reduction in discretionary fixed costs could reduce the total fixed costs by $87,343. This could cushion the budget enough to afford potential service fluctuations or unexpected cost increases.
![Bridgestone Discretionary Fixed Costs](/assets/images/bridge-discretionary-fixed-costs.png)
![Bridgestone Fixed Expenses](/assets/images/bridge-fixed-expense.png)
## Cuts to Services
Service Cuts in the most medically intensive or lowest Contribution Margin Ratio Services would allow for significant cuts to the budgeted Salary & Benefits fixed costs, which is currently a significant portion of total fixed costs, $1,827,607. Medical Somatic Treatments have a negative contribution margin, but they are a key component of Bridgestones Services and are integral to the overall success of the many of the services offered. Intensive Outpatient and Methadone Maintenance, other low CM ratio revenue might be outsourced if possible in order for the clinic and crucial services were threatened.
![Bridgestone Cuts](/assets/images/bridge-cuts.png)
## Recommendations
Bridgestone should take the budget route that causes the least disruption to services. Their
current services offered provides comprehensive care for the complicated mental health
issues for people with drug addiction and mental health diagnosis. It is recommended that
Bridgestone reduces it’s discretionary fixed costs to allow for more room for unexpected
fluctuations in the budget.
